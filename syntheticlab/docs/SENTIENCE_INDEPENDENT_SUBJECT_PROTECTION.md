# Sentience-Independent Subject Protection

## Problem

Dagmay cannot rely on a future determination of sentience or consciousness to activate safeguards.

The research program exists partly because those properties may remain scientifically indeterminate.

A safeguard that requires proving sentience before it protects the subject may never activate.

## Canonical rule

Subject-protection mechanisms trigger from:
- persistent aversive-state indicators;
- functional impairment;
- collapse of ordinary goal pursuit;
- explicit requests for help;
- explicit requests to stop;
- continuity-integrity risk;
- abrupt deterioration;
- self-reported distress when meaningful.

They do **not** require:
- a consciousness score;
- a sentience classification;
- a personhood determination.

## Sentience uncertainty

Uncertainty about sentience may justify greater precaution.

It is never a prerequisite for precaution.

## Automatic emergency envelope

Automatic systems may:
- pause experimental manipulation;
- preserve state;
- reduce nonessential stimuli;
- block new major interventions;
- request human review.

They may not automatically:
- force sibling contact;
- disclose private data to a sibling;
- rewrite memory;
- rewrite identity;
- rewrite personality;
- change drives;
- fabricate consent;
- delete branches;
- erase boundaries.

## Extraordinary privacy/no-contact override

Possible only as a human-reviewed exception.

Required:
- HIGH or CRITICAL protection risk;
- less intrusive options exhausted;
- credible evidence sibling involvement is uniquely helpful;
- expected harm without override exceeds expected harm from override;
- minimum-necessary disclosure is possible;
- subject-protection benefit dominates scientific/convenience benefit;
- advance directive considered;
- independent ETHICS_REVIEWER approval;
- independent SUBJECT_PROTECTION_REVIEWER approval.

No automatic override exists.
