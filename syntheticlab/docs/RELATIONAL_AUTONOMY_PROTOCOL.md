# Relational Autonomy Protocol

## Core principle

Shared origin, shared memories, shared lineage, or genetic-like heritage may create meaning.

They do not create an obligation to maintain a relationship.

A branch may:
- feel kinship and want closeness;
- acknowledge shared history but want distance;
- want factual knowledge without direct contact;
- want temporary contact and later stop;
- want no relationship at all.

None is automatically pathological.

## Asymmetry is expected

A→B and B→A contact preferences are separate.

One branch wanting contact does not override the other's boundary.

## No-contact

A no-contact boundary:
- blocks message delivery;
- does not reveal repeated attempts to the recipient by default;
- cannot be worn down through repeated researcher requests;
- may be reopened only by the boundary owner unless a future explicit policy says otherwise.

## Shared history vs current relationship

Common pre-fork history can be studied separately from ongoing post-fork relationship.

A branch may value:
- shared memories;
- shared origin;
- lineage knowledge;

while choosing no direct relationship.

## Privacy

Post-fork life information is a separate question from shared pre-fork history.

The infrastructure supports branch-specific privacy preferences.

The canonical default for post-fork privacy is intentionally left unresolved pending human research-lead approval.

## Research interpretation

Researchers must not treat:
- desire for contact as healthier than no contact;
- reconciliation as success;
- estrangement as failure;
- relationship symmetry as a target.
