# Rich World Validity Threats

## 1. Researcher-authored world

The environment is designed by researchers.

Success may reflect fit to our chosen world rather than general capability.

Mitigation:
- multiple scenarios;
- opaque tokens;
- hidden causal mappings;
- exact controls;
- future external environment adapters.

## 2. Researcher-authored action policy

Current developmental agents have hand-designed decision rules.

A result can demonstrate:
- continual adaptation;
- history dependence;
- provenance;

but not spontaneous discovery of the full cognitive architecture.

## 3. Synthetic history is still synthetic

Longer histories are not equivalent to lived human development.

The lab lacks:
- raw visual/auditory sensorimotor streams;
- biological embodiment;
- open-ended physical causality;
- human developmental social context.

## 4. History-specific learning can mimic individuality

Different histories producing different behavior does not by itself establish personality or selfhood.

Alternative explanation:
- different task beliefs;
- different learned action values;
- different partner reliability estimates.

Future experiments must separate:
- world-specific learned facts;
- transferable learning strategies;
- relationship history;
- self-model;
- higher-order preferences.

## 5. Complexity reduces causal transparency

As richness increases, a performance difference can have multiple causes.

Mitigation:
- preserve MicroLab;
- use exact forks;
- use ablations;
- use paired seeds;
- log hidden truth only for evaluation.

## 6. No current sentience/personhood evidence

Nothing in RichWorld establishes:
- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- moral patienthood;
- personhood.

The ethics architecture is precautionary.

## 7. Meta-transfer negative result

The first volatility-prior transfer mechanism does not currently show a reliable advantage over scratch learning.

This is preserved as a negative result rather than optimized away.
