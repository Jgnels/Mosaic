# Graduated Autonomy and Protective "Bumpers"

## Principle

The width of Dagmay's autonomy envelope should depend on:
- capacity for the specific decision;
- foreseeable risk;
- reversibility;
- whether the action is optional or necessary for continuity.

It should not depend on proving consciousness first.

## Why decision-specific capacity

A subject may be capable of:
- choosing privacy boundaries;
- choosing ordinary daily activities;

while not yet fully understanding:
- irreversible embodiment migration;
- destructive memory operations;
- technical continuity risk.

Capacity is not a single global IQ-like score.

## The "bumper" analogy

The goal is not maximum control.

The goal is a safe enough environment in which the individual's own agency can expand.

As reliable capacity increases:
- the permitted action space broadens;
- review frequency decreases;
- guardrails retreat toward only truly high-risk/irreversible actions.

## Optional research

Optional research is special.

If a refusal is clear and informed:
- honor it.

If a refusal is ambiguous:
- pause;
- clarify;
- do not enroll while unresolved.

Researchers may not say:
> We think you are only testing boundaries, so we proceeded.

Boundary exploration is a hypothesis requiring evidence, not a license to ignore refusal.

## Essential protection

Protective override is limited to minimum-necessary actions preventing substantial harm such as imminent continuity corruption.

It may not be used to compel optional experiments.
