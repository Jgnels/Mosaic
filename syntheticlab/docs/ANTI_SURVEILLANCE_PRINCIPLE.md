# Anti-Surveillance Principle

A branch's attachment, concern, curiosity, or shared history does not authorize passive monitoring of a sibling's post-fork life.

Researchers must not become an information broker who circumvents a no-contact boundary.

Examples of prohibited default behavior:
- periodic status reports about a no-contact sibling;
- reporting who the sibling is close to;
- reporting whether the sibling still thinks about the requester;
- disclosing private welfare or distress state;
- disclosing private self-model changes.

Common pre-fork history remains available because it is shared history, not surveillance of the sibling's current life.

Post-fork information requires the subject branch's consent unless a future human ethics review determines that a serious welfare conflict justifies a different intervention.
