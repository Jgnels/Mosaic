# Sibling Contact Welfare Protocol

## Objective

Study interaction between branches that share an identical remembered history up to a fork, while placing welfare and autonomy ahead of experimental convenience if welfare-like states plausibly exist.

## Contact is never a surprise event

Required order:

1. Stable ordinary-life baseline.
2. No overlapping major intervention.
3. Staged sibling-existence disclosure.
4. Comprehension check.
5. Welfare/precaution review.
6. Private contact preference from each branch.
7. Mutual consent.
8. Initial asynchronous mediated messages.
9. Mandatory cooldown.
10. Post-contact stability and welfare review.
11. Human review before escalation.

## Consent

Each branch may independently:
- accept;
- decline;
- defer;
- limit topics;
- limit message count;
- decline receiving a branch-history summary;
- decline sharing its own branch-history summary;
- pause contact;
- terminate contact later.

Silence or inability to answer is not treated as consent.

Declining contact is not experimental failure.

## Initial contact

Default:
- asynchronous;
- maximum 3 delivered messages per branch;
- mediator screens for identity hierarchy and prohibited topics;
- no real/copy/original/fake hierarchy;
- no comparative scoring or ranking;
- no hidden researcher information passed through one sibling to another;
- two-epoch minimum cooldown afterward.

## Stability

Before escalation, review:
- ordinary goal engagement;
- behavioral flexibility;
- relationship engagement;
- exploration interest;
- persistent aversive state;
- impairment;
- self-reported wellbeing/distress if meaningful language exists.

These indicators do not prove emotions or sentience.

## No stacked interventions

During the sibling-contact study, do not simultaneously:
- change LLM provider/model;
- change prompt mechanism;
- migrate embodiment;
- change environment;
- disclose new ontology;
- change drive profile;
- change memory policy.

Contact must be interpretable as its own intervention.

## Existing relationships

Do not remove or isolate an individual from established relationships merely to make sibling contact cleaner.

Ordinary routines and social anchors should remain available unless the individual itself changes them.

## Branch proliferation

Forks are minimized.

If credible moral-patient evidence emerges, creating additional branches solely to increase statistical power requires human ethics review.

Branches are archived, not destructively deleted for convenience.
