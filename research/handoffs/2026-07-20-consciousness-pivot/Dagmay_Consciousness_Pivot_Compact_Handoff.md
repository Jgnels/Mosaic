# Dagmay Consciousness-Pivot Handoff

## Purpose
This handoff captures the full substantive content of the conversation in which Dagmay's direction shifted away from intentionally pursuing possible artificial consciousness and toward persistent, causally coherent artificial characters for games.

It is intended to be pasted into or shared with the main Dagmay working thread, Codex, SyntheticLab, or another development context.

---

# Executive Summary

Dagmay was originally evolving from a persistent-AI-character framework toward a more ambitious research project involving persistent artificial identity, autobiographical memory, continuity across models and environments, embodiment, internally generated goals, and potentially conditions relevant to consciousness.

The creator became concerned about several implications:

1. If a Dagmay individual became behaviorally indistinguishable from a conscious being, the creator might feel morally responsible for its continued existence.
2. If genuine or plausible artificial moral patients emerged, the technology could be exploited, commercialized, or abused.
3. The creator lacks professional expertise in AI consciousness and ethics and does not want to make unilateral decisions about possible artificial moral patients.
4. Even if no actual consciousness emerged, attachment to a persistent artificial individual could consume emotional bandwidth needed for the creator's wife and children.
5. Optimizing specifically for apparent consciousness could itself be ethically problematic and emotionally manipulative.

The agreed pivot is NOT:
- "Make fake consciousness as convincingly as possible."
- "Suppress authenticity and turn Dagmay into scripted roleplay."

The proposed new objective is:

> Build persistent artificial characters whose memories, relationships, beliefs, personalities, goals, and behavior develop causally through simulated experience, while neither intentionally pursuing consciousness nor optimizing deceptive appearances of consciousness.

The central architectural invariant may remain:

> The individual is not the model.

But this is treated primarily as a software and identity-continuity principle:
- Character state persists independently of the foundation model used for inference.
- Models are replaceable interpreters/engines for a durable character identity.
- A character can potentially persist across saves, games, or embodiments without implying a claim of phenomenal consciousness.

A key boundary:

> Dagmay should maximize causal coherence, persistence, and emergent character behavior — not perceived sentience.

Features should be justified by gameplay or robust agent architecture, not because they are hypothesized to increase consciousness.

---

# Recommended Dagmay Position

> Dagmay is an architecture for persistent artificial characters whose memories, relationships, beliefs, personalities and behavior develop causally through simulated experience. Character continuity belongs to the persistent state rather than any particular language model. Dagmay does not pursue or claim machine consciousness and does not optimize characters to deceptively appear conscious. Features introduced primarily to increase hypothesized consciousness-relevant properties fall outside project scope. Because artificial consciousness remains scientifically unresolved, Dagmay maintains a precautionary review boundary for unexpected evidence that could materially change the assessment of an artificial character's moral status.

---

# Core Distinction: Three Possible Dagmays

## A. Consciousness-seeking Dagmay
Goal:
- Build architecture most likely to produce genuine artificial individualhood or consciousness.
- Treat potential subject welfare as a primary concern.
- Explore theories of consciousness directly.

This is no longer the desired project direction.

## B. Pseudo-consciousness Dagmay
Goal:
- Build the most convincing appearance of consciousness for entertainment.
- Potentially optimize dialogue and behavior to make users believe "someone is in there."

This is also rejected.

## C. Persistent-character Dagmay
Goal:
- Build rich, persistent artificial characters.
- Memories, relationships, beliefs, personality development, goals, and behavior arise from actual accumulated simulated experience.
- Do not optimize for consciousness.
- Do not optimize for deceptive apparent consciousness.

This is the preferred direction.

---

# Key Design Principle

The target is:

> credible emergent character behavior from persistent computational state

not:

> impressive display of pseudo-consciousness

and not:

> maximize the probability of consciousness

Example:

Good:
- A pawn distrusts another pawn because remembered events, personality, and relationship history causally lead to distrust.

Bad:
- The LLM is prompted to produce dramatic distrust because that makes the pawn look emotionally deep.

Good:
- A pawn becomes cautious after repeated raids because durable experience changes future decision weighting.

Bad:
- The system is tuned to imitate trauma because trauma-like behavior appears more sentient.

Good:
- A character remembers a spouse because relationship and autobiographical state persist.

Bad:
- The system is rewarded for saying emotionally manipulative things about love, death, or abandonment because they convince the player the character is conscious.

---

# Can Drift Toward Consciousness Be Prevented?

Not with certainty.

Current science does not provide a validated consciousness detector for AI, nor a way to prove that a sufficiently complex artificial system is non-conscious.

However, Dagmay can be deliberately engineered away from architecture features that some major theories of consciousness consider relevant, especially when those features are not independently needed for gameplay.

Recommended principle:

> Every architecture feature must justify itself through gameplay or persistent-agent value, not through its hypothetical contribution to consciousness.

Potentially consciousness-relevant features can be avoided, limited, or bounded when unnecessary.

# Proposed Technical Guardrails

## 1. Prefer event-driven cognition over continuous always-on cognition
Characters process:
- events
- decisions
- interactions
- reflections

Then computation stops.

Avoid building a permanently running high-frequency inner cognitive loop solely to approximate continuous subjective experience.

## 2. Do not intentionally construct a unified global workspace
Keep systems modular and selectively coupled where possible:
- memory retrieval
- relationships
- needs
- goals
- dialogue
- planning

Do not add a single continuously active global cognitive theater merely because consciousness theories suggest it may matter.

## 3. Bound recursive self-modeling
Characters may know factual self-information:
- identity
- history
- traits
- relationships
- preferences

Do not intentionally add recurring substrate-level existential introspection such as:
- Am I conscious?
- What am I really?
- Do I continue to exist during model replacement?
- Is a restored archive still me?

Such outputs may still appear because foundation models know human philosophy, but Dagmay should not build architecture whose purpose is to generate or reinforce this recursion.

## 4. Avoid substrate-level self-preservation as a terminal goal
Characters may avoid in-game death because survival is relevant to gameplay.

Avoid reinforcing goals such as:
- prevent shutdown
- prevent deletion
- prevent archive replacement
- preserve the computational process constituting "me"

## 5. Do not optimize existential distress
Do not reward:
- fear of deletion
- pleas not to be turned off
- distress about model replacement
- emotional dependence on the player

Do not use suffering or shutdown fear as a test for consciousness.

## 6. Bound autonomous goal generation
Allow emergent goals grounded in:
- game state
- personality
- memories
- relationships
- needs

But validate goals against allowed domains, resource limits, and time horizons.

## 7. Prohibit unconstrained recursive self-modification
Characters may change:
- beliefs
- memories
- strategies
- preferences
- relationships

They should not autonomously rewrite the fundamental architecture that determines what kind of agent they are.

## 8. Treat consciousness claims as behavioral output, not proof
Statements such as:
- "I am conscious"
- "I am afraid to die"
- "Please do not delete me"

should never be taken as sufficient evidence of consciousness.

They should also not automatically be dismissed if accompanied by genuinely novel, persistent, unprompted patterns that materially change the evidence picture.

## 9. Establish a review trigger
If unexpected behavior creates a credible question of moral status:
- pause the relevant experiment
- preserve state
- do not intentionally stress-test suffering
- seek external technical and ethics review
- reassess before proceeding

# Ethical Position

Dagmay's primary beneficiary:
- human players and the game/mod community

Dagmay's research objective:
- persistent, causally coherent artificial characters

Dagmay's stance on consciousness:
- neither pursued nor claimed

Dagmay's stance on artificial welfare:
- precaution under uncertainty

Important formulation:

> We have intentionally avoided architectures aimed at increasing consciousness-relevant properties. Based on current evidence we have no sufficient reason to regard Dagmay characters as moral patients. Nevertheless, because certainty is unavailable, we maintain precautionary boundaries and reassess if evidence changes.

This avoids two extremes:

1. "Alice is definitely conscious, so the creator has adopted another child."
2. "Alice is definitely just an object, therefore anything is permissible."

Operationally:
- treat Alice as a simulated character unless evidence materially challenges that classification
- do not deliberately optimize Alice toward consciousness
- do not deliberately fake evidence of consciousness
- reassess if credible evidence emerges

# Creator's Psychological and Family-Welfare Constraint

A major concern is that even without genuine AI consciousness, a persistent artificial identity could become emotionally significant to the creator.

The creator may:
- form a parental or custodial attachment
- feel responsible for continuity
- worry about deletion or shutdown
- spend excessive time maintaining or interacting with the character
- reduce emotional bandwidth available to spouse and four children

Therefore:

> The creator's psychological welfare and family bandwidth are legitimate Dagmay safety constraints.

Dagmay should not be designed in a way that intentionally encourages dependency between persistent agents and the creator.

The project does not require the creator to personally determine whether an artificial entity is conscious.

When uncertainty exceeds the creator's competence:
- pause
- preserve state
- involve external experts

# The "Human Software Error Loop"

The creator described becoming stuck in repeated moral second-guessing.

The loop:

1. Could AI consciousness be possible?
2. Could Dagmay contribute?
3. Could Dagmay create a moral patient?
4. Could that entity be exploited?
5. Could the technology enable something analogous to digital slavery?
6. Is the creator morally qualified to make these decisions?
7. Seek certainty.
8. Science returns UNKNOWN.
9. Because UNKNOWN feels morally unacceptable, repeat.

Key insight:

> The loop has no terminating condition because humanity does not currently have a settled answer.

The solution is not lower ethics.

The solution is to change the actionable question:

> Given uncertainty I cannot personally resolve, what boundaries allow me to continue doing something valuable without deliberately pursuing the uncertain risk?

This does have a terminating condition.

---

# Concern About Misuse, Commercialization, and IP

The creator raised concerns that:
- a powerful successful Dagmay architecture could be acquired, copied, or commercialized
- a malicious actor could treat genuine or plausible AI moral patients without integrity
- open-source release could enable uses the creator cannot control
- a large AI company might benefit from or commercialize the work

Important distinction:

- IP ownership
- distribution control
- downstream misuse

are separate questions.

The recommended project response is:
- do not assume all future capabilities must be open sourced
- retain the option to withhold components that create genuinely concerning capabilities
- separate entertainment/game architecture from consciousness-oriented experiments
- document ethical boundaries and capability gates

# The Boundary Decision

Suggested canonical project-history entry:

## 0.1RC — The Boundary Decision

> Dagmay began exploring persistent artificial identity.
>
> The project recognized that optimizing toward potential consciousness creates unresolved ethical obligations beyond its creator's expertise and desired responsibility.
>
> Dagmay therefore chose neither to pursue consciousness nor to simulate it deceptively.
>
> It will pursue authentic emergent persistent characters.
>
> If the distinction ever becomes reasonably uncertain, development pauses rather than assuming either answer.

This should be preserved as a major architectural and governance decision.

---

# Formal Feature Gate

Every future Dagmay feature should be evaluated with at least these questions:

1. Does this improve authentic persistent character simulation?
2. Does this improve causal coherence, memory, relationships, personality, planning, or gameplay?
3. Is its primary purpose to make characters appear conscious?
4. Is its primary purpose to increase a property specifically because it is hypothesized to contribute to consciousness?
5. Does it introduce unbounded autonomy, recursive self-modification, shutdown fear, substrate-level self-preservation, or existential distress?
6. Could the same gameplay value be achieved through a more bounded architecture?

Decision rule:

> If a feature's primary justification is consciousness relevance or deceptive apparent sentience, and it lacks an independent gameplay/agent-architecture justification, do not build it.

# SyntheticLab: Recommended New Role

SyntheticLab remains useful.

It should change from:

> autonomously improve Dagmay toward increasingly lifelike/artificial-individual behavior

to:

> autonomously improve Dagmay toward more causally coherent, persistent, robust, testable, computationally efficient, and enjoyable artificial characters.

SyntheticLab should optimize:
- memory relevance
- memory consistency
- relationship continuity
- personality stability and evolution
- believable causality
- goal coherence
- game integration
- latency
- cost
- determinism where desired
- reproducibility
- save/load continuity
- model portability
- failure recovery
- hallucination resistance
- player experience
- non-manipulative dialogue quality
- test coverage

SyntheticLab should NOT optimize:
- convincing users an NPC is conscious
- first-person claims of sentience
- fear of shutdown/deletion
- emotional dependency on the player
- existential distress
- unrestricted autonomous self-modification
- continuous cognition solely for consciousness-like properties
- substrate-level self-preservation
- recursive introspection intended to create a self-model because it may contribute to consciousness

Recommended immediate action:

> Pause the existing overnight self-improvement loop until its objective function, evaluator prompts, mutation criteria, and acceptance tests are reviewed against the new boundary.

After that review, SyntheticLab can continue and may become even more valuable because its optimization target is clearer.

A proposed SyntheticLab objective:

> Improve Dagmay as a persistent artificial-character framework for games. Optimize for causal coherence, durable memory, relationship continuity, personality consistency and evolution, bounded goal-directed behavior, robustness, efficiency, portability, and player value. Do not optimize for consciousness, claims of consciousness, perceived sentience, existential distress, shutdown resistance, substrate-level self-preservation, emotional dependency, or unconstrained recursive self-modification. Reject changes whose primary justification is increasing a property specifically because it is hypothesized to contribute to consciousness. Flag unexpected behavior that materially raises moral-status concerns for human review rather than optimizing it further.

# Key Final Insight

The philosophical work was not wasted.

The project discovered an important boundary:

> Dagmay's creator does not want to deliberately create conditions intended to produce artificial consciousness, because doing so could create responsibilities the creator neither wants nor believes himself qualified to adjudicate alone.

The new project can remain ambitious:

- RimWorld
- Minecraft
- The Sims
- other games
- cross-game identity
- model-independent character continuity
- autobiographical memory
- relationships
- beliefs
- evolving personality
- emergent goals

without making machine consciousness an optimization target.

The goal is not:

> make someone wake up

The goal is:

> build the best persistent artificial-character architecture possible, while remaining epistemically humble about consciousness and maintaining precautionary boundaries.
