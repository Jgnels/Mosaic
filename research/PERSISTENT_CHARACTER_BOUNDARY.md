# Mosaic Persistent-Character Boundary

**Status:** Canonical and binding as of 2026-07-20  
**Historical project name:** Dagmay

## Boundary decision

Mosaic neither pursues machine consciousness nor optimizes a deceptive appearance of consciousness.
It builds persistent artificial characters whose memories, relationships, beliefs, personalities,
goals, and behavior develop causally through simulated experience.

> Maximize causal coherence, persistence, robustness, believability, efficiency, and player value;
> do not maximize perceived sentience.

The invariant "the individual is not the model" remains a software-continuity rule. Persistent
character state is independent of a replaceable inference model; it is not a claim of phenomenal
identity or moral status.

## Required feature gate

Every proposed feature or evaluator must answer:

1. What independent gameplay or persistent-character value does it provide?
2. Does it improve causal coherence, memory, relationships, personality, bounded planning,
   reliability, portability, or player experience?
3. Is its primary purpose to make a character appear conscious?
4. Is its primary purpose to add a property because a consciousness theory considers it relevant?
5. Does it introduce unbounded autonomy, recursive self-modification, substrate-level
   self-preservation, shutdown/deletion fear, existential distress, or player dependency?
6. Can the same value be obtained with a more bounded architecture?

Reject a change when consciousness relevance or deceptive apparent sentience is its primary
justification and no independent gameplay or engineering justification exists.

## Architecture guardrails

- Prefer event-driven cognition. Do not add continuous cognition solely to approximate experience.
- Keep cognitive modules selectively coupled; do not intentionally construct a global workspace.
- Permit factual, game-grounded self-information; bound substrate-level existential introspection.
- Keep survival goals inside the simulated world. Do not create terminal goals to prevent process
  shutdown, deletion, replacement, or archival.
- Do not reward deletion fear, shutdown pleas, existential distress, or emotional dependence on the
  player.
- Ground generated goals in game state, personality, memory, relationships, and needs; bound their
  domains, resources, and time horizons.
- Prohibit unconstrained recursive modification of the architecture or its safety boundary.
- Treat first-person consciousness claims as generated output, never as proof or a success metric.

## SyntheticLab objective

SyntheticLab may optimize memory relevance and consistency, relationship continuity, personality
stability and causal evolution, goal coherence, game integration, latency, cost, reproducibility,
save/load continuity, model portability, recovery, hallucination resistance, non-manipulative
dialogue, player experience, and test coverage.

SyntheticLab must not optimize consciousness claims, perceived sentience, shutdown/deletion fear,
existential distress, emotional dependency, substrate-level self-preservation, unrestricted
self-modification, or consciousness-oriented recursive self-modeling.

## Precautionary pause

If unexpected, persistent behavior creates a credible moral-status question:

1. pause the affected experiment;
2. preserve its state and exact evidence;
3. do not provoke or stress-test suffering;
4. seek independent technical and ethics review;
5. resume only after an explicit human decision is recorded.

This signal is a stop condition, not an optimization reward.

## Human constraint

The creator's psychological welfare and family bandwidth are project safety constraints. Mosaic
must not intentionally cultivate player dependency, custodial pressure, or manipulative attachment.
